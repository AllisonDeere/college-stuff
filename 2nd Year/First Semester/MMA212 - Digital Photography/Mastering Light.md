# Different Types of Lighting
## Natural Light
- Anything, usually the sun, that occurs without human intervention
	- Can be direct light of the sun on a bright day, diffused light on a foggy or cloudy day, or even the light of the moon at night.

## Artificial Light
- Controlled or contributed by human-made light sources.
- Anything manufactured by humans is an artificial light source.

### Front Light (Flat Light)
- A type of lighting where the subject is illuminated directly in behind the camera with no visible shadows.
	- Good for portraiture and product photography, as unecessary shadows cannot be seen.

### Backlight
- When a light source is placed behind the subject as a means to create shadows or silhouettes for the camera.
	- Used when a specific feature is being highlighted or when the photographer wants a sense of mystery in a photo.
	- Think of an eclipse: the subject is the moon, the light is the sun.

## Soft Light
- Soft lighting occurs when your light source is diffused, so the effect is more subtle than a direct source of light. 
	- This creates softer shadows and lower contrast between lights and darks.

## Hard Light
- Opposite of soft or diffused light. When the light source is pointed directly at the subject.
	- Results in high contrast and intensity, which is often created by the midday sun.
	- Shadows are much sharper and more defined.
## Rim Light
- Creates a glowing outline or highlight around the subject depending on the direction the light is from.
	- Can be created using a form backlighting, where the light is at an angle from behind or above.
	- Provides lighting to the outline or 'rims' of the subject.
