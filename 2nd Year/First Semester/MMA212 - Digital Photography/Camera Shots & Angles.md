# Camera Shot
- A camera shot is composed of the series of frames that are shot uninterrupted from the moment the camera starts rolling until it stops. Camera shots are essential in filmmaking and video productions, by combining different types of shots, angles and camera movements the filmmakers are able to do different things.
## Focal Length
- Distance between the modal point and the sensor.
## Shot Size 
- How much the subject and surrounding environment is captured in a frame.
	- The ratio between subject and environment.
		- From widest to narrow:
			- **Extreme Long Shot/Extreme Wide Shot (ELS/EWS)**
				- An extreme long/wide shot makes your subject appear small against their location. Used to make your subject feel distant or unfamiliar.
			- **Long Shot / Wide Shot (LS/WS)**
				- LS/WS is the same idea, but a bit closer. If your subject is a person then their body will be in full view. There should be a good deal of space above and below the subject. 
			- **Full** 
				- This allows the subject to fill the frame while keeping emphasis on scenery. Can be used to feature multiple subjects in a single shot.
			- **Medium Wide/Long Shot** 
				- Splits the difference between a full shot and a medium shot. Captures the subject from **the knees up**.
			- **Cowboy**
				- Mid-thighs up. Used in westerns to frame a gunslinger's gun or holster on his hip.
			- **Medium** 
				- From the waist up, through the torso. Arguably the most common camera shot.
			- **Medium closeup**
				- Chest-up. Favors the face but still keeps the subject distance.
			- **Closeup**
				- Neck to top of the head. To highlight emotions and reactions.
			- **Extreme closeup**
				- The most you can fill a frame with your subject. Eyes, mouths, and gun triggers. In extreme close-up shots, smaller objects get great detail and are the focal point.

# Camera Angles
- The camera shot angle is used to specify the location where the camera is placed to take a shot. The position of the camera in relation to the subjects can affect the way the viewer perceives the scene.
	- **Eye Level Shot**
		-  Neutral perspective, mimics how we see people in real life. Our eye line is connecting with theirs.
	- **Low Angle Shot**
		- Camera looks up at the subject. These camera shots most often emphasize power dynamics between characters.
	- **High Angle Shot**
		- Camera points down at the subject. Creates inferiority.
	- **Hip Level Shot**
		- Camera height is roughly waist-high.
	- **Knee Level Shot**
		- When your camera height is about as low as your subject's knees. Can emphasizes superiority.
