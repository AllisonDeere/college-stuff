Climate change is a big problem for the global north and south, since we all live on a single planet. One of the most vulnurable and arguably THE most vulnurable country affected by climate change however, is Bangladesh. Now, climate change by definition, is the result of global greenhouse gas emissions from non-renewable sources of energy such as coal and oil, rising sea levels caused by glaciers and ice melting, deforestation reduces the planet's natural carbon absorption, and industrialization and urbanization increases our energy consumption. Bangladesh is a geographically small country with about only 148 thousand square kilometers, but it is the eight most populous nation in the world with almost 176 million residents.

Speaking of geography, much of Bangladesh is close to sea level, which means that when the sea levels rise, and flooding, typhoons, and other rainfall related stuff hits Bangladesh, which they do often as frequent as us in the Philippines,  they are pretty heavily impacted, considering that millions of people are coastal and river-bound, which means they live close to water, and one of the disadvantages of Bangladesh is that they are agriculturally dependent, so any climate disasters threaten their food security by a lot. When you take the food away from a developing country, who has very little resources to offer to protect itself from natural disasters, climate change can have big effects on the economy, the infrastructure and their development. Bangladesh is one of the countries that contributes very little to greenhouse-gas emissions, that being a good thing of course, but they can experience some of the greatest consequences of climate change.
